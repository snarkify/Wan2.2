1. Executive summary

Verified at the CUDA-kernel level: inside Wan2.2's 14B DiT forward at 1280×720, self-attention dominates the per-step budget but not as much as the back-of-envelope predicted, and three secondary costs are larger than expected.

The "SDPA fallback" was a misleading label. The dominant attention kernel is cudnn_generated_fort_native_sdpa_sm90_flash_fprop_wgmma_f16_* — cuDNN's FlashAttention-style implementation using Hopper's WGMMA instruction, achieving ~560 TFLOPS sustained. We've been running an FA-class kernel all along. This shrinks the realistic FA3 upgrade win from "~140 s" (original estimate vs naive SDPA) to ~100-130 s (vs cuDNN's already-FA2-class kernel).
FFN matmul achieves only 27% of H100's BF16 peak. Two nvjet_sm90_*_TNT kernels (the FFN up/down projections) eat 16.6% of the budget at ~205 TFLOPS sustained out of ~750 TFLOPS dense peak. This is the regime where FP8 quantization has outsized leverage — same arithmetic intensity, half the bytes through HBM. If FP8 lifts these kernels to 50% of FP8 peak, we'd save ~5 s/step × 40 = 200 s on the diffusion loop.
Step 26's extra 27 s is unpinned PCIe memcpy, not compute. At the boundary timestep, Wan2.2 swaps high-noise → low-noise DiT via pageable host memory (~2 GB/s), costing 13.4 s DtoH + 13.3 s HtoD = ~27 s for 54 GB of model weights moved. Either dropping --offload_model True (already proved bit-equal via Y4-no-offload, 0 s) or calling .pin_memory() on the CPU-resident DiT weights (~22-25 s saved) eliminates it.
Step 0's extra 9.4 s is cuBLAS/cuDNN autotune, not compute. GPU kernel mix on step 0 is byte-for-byte identical to steady-state; the extra time is in CPU-side first-call kernel selection that gets memoized for the remaining 39 steps. Unavoidable, but tiny in absolute terms.

2. Why Track A & how it's measured

The multi-engine benchmark report showed that on 2× H100, our in-tree CFG-parallel-2 + sync fix (Y4-sync) reaches 613 s diffusion at 2.64× single-GPU. That number is the total — but where, inside the 11.92-second-per-step DiT forward, does the time go? Is it attention? MLP? LayerNorm? Memory ops?

The earlier coarse trace (Wan's custom span profiler) gives us "model_forward_cfg_split is 12 s/step" but no breakdown inside. Track A's job: replace the back-of-envelope estimate with measured ground truth, identify the actual bottleneck, and surface non-obvious costs (the boundary swap, the warmup overhead) so future optimization is targeted at real bottlenecks instead of imagined ones.

How the measurement was taken

A small env-gated patch to wan/text2video.py wraps the diffusion loop in torch.profiler.profile() with a schedule that captures exactly a few diffusion steps (skips warmup noise, records the steady-state / warmup / boundary as needed). The loop early-exits after capturing — no VAE decode, no full mp4 — so each run finishes in ~12 min on a single H100. Default behavior is unchanged when WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS isn't set. 

# Three runs, each ~12 min on 1× H100. Targets selected via env-flag.
WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS=1 WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS_TARGET=steady    python generate.py ...
WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS=1 WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS_TARGET=warmup    python generate.py ...
WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS=1 WAN_PROFILE_KERNELS_TARGET=boundary  python generate.py ...

Each trace is a Chrome / Perfetto JSON ~30-75 MB. A post-processing script, scripts/profiling/analyze_kernel_trace.py, buckets every GPU kernel into semantic categories (attention_math, matmul, layernorm, elementwise, etc.) by regex on kernel name, and reports per-category totals + the top individual kernels + a side-by-side delta vs the analytical prediction. See §11 for full reproduction details.

5. Achieved throughput vs H100 peak

Knowing the per-category time isn't enough to decide where to optimize — we also need to know how close each category is to hardware limits. If a category is already at 80% of peak, there's not much headroom; if it's at 25%, there's a lot of room for kernel improvements (FP8, fusion, better tile selection).

CategoryTotal FLOPS in 2 steady steps Wall timeAchieved % of H100 peakHeadroom verdict
Attention math4 × 4,680 TFLOPS = 18,720 TFLOPS 33.4 s~560 TFLOPS ~70% of FA peak FA-class; FA3 may lift to ~800 TFLOPS (+1.4×)
Matmul (FFN + proj)4 × 588 TFLOPS = 2,352 TFLOPS 11.5 s~205 TFLOPS 27% of dense peak (750) Lots of headroom — FP8 doubles bytes-per-flop, easy 1.5-2× win
Elementwise (residual, modulation, activation)bandwidth-bound 10.5 s~0.5 TB/s effective ~15% of 3.35 TB/s Fusion-bound — many small kernels each at 1-3% peak BW; torch.compile's reduce-overhead mode would help if determinism issues were solved
LayerNormbandwidth-bound (~5 GB) 0.74 s~7 GB/s effective ~0.2% of peak Already vectorized; fusion with adjacent residual could halve the cost (~0.4 s/step) but not worth the complexity

Two clean stories from this table:

  • Attention math is the biggest absolute cost (33.4 s / 59%) and is already running well (70% of FA peak). FA3 would lift the efficiency further, but the kernel is already in good shape.
  • FFN matmul is the highest-leverage category — 27% of peak means there's a 2-3× theoretical improvement available with the right quantization (FP8). On the kernel total of 11.5 s that's 4-7 s/step, or ~200-280 s saved over 40 steps.

6. Step 0 (warmup) — the cuBLAS autotune story

Step 0 takes 23.7 s wall vs steady-state's 14.4 s — an extra 9.4 s. What's taking the extra time? Captured a separate trace specifically of step 0.

Result: GPU kernels are byte-for-byte identical to steady-state

CategoryStep 0 (warmup)Steady (steps 3-4)Δ
attention_math58.9%59.2%-0.3
matmul20.4%20.3%+0.1
elementwise18.9%18.7%+0.2
layernorm1.3%1.3%0.0
other0.5%0.5%0.0

Same kernels, same proportions. The extra 9.4 s of wall time is therefore not GPU compute. CPU-side analysis: the dispatcher events for aten::scaled_dot_product_attention, aten::linear, and aten::addmm are all 2-3× their steady-state duration — consistent with cuBLAS/cuDNN running their first-call autotune to pick the optimal kernel variant for each unique input shape. Wan2.2's DiT has ~40+ distinct matmul shapes (different head splits, FFN sizes, modulation projections, etc.); each pays a small autotune cost on first call, then the choice is memoized for steps 1-39.

Implication: step 0 overhead is unavoidable but tiny in absolute terms (1.5% of the 613 s diffusion). Not worth optimizing. It would only become meaningful in a heavily-batched serving setup where many short requests cycle through, in which case "warm the kernel cache once" at server init time would amortize it.

7. Step 26 (boundary swap) — the PCIe pageable-memcpy story

Step 26 takes 41.3 s wall vs 14.4 s steady — an extra 27 s. At step 26 the diffusion timestep crosses boundary_ratio × num_train_timesteps = 875, which is Wan2.2's signal to switch from the high-noise DiT to the low-noise DiT. With --offload_model True, that means a model swap: high-noise goes to CPU memory, low-noise comes off CPU memory to GPU. Where does the 27 s go?

Result: 28 s of unpinned-pageable PCIe memcpy

Captured steps 25-26 specifically. GPU kernel mix is still identical to steady-state (59% / 20% / 19% / 1%). The extra time appears in a different category: gpu_memcpy.

EventTotal timeNotes
Memcpy DtoH (Device → Pageable host)13.42 sEvict high-noise DiT (27 GB) from GPU back to CPU
Memcpy HtoD (Pageable host → Device)13.31 sLoad low-noise DiT (27 GB) onto GPU
Memcpy DtoD1.31 sInternal allocator reshuffling
cudaMemsetAsync0.27 sZero-fill freed regions
Total memcpy~28 sMatches step 26's extra ~27 s wall

Why so slow? Pageable memory throttles PCIe

H100 supports PCIe Gen5 at theoretical ~64 GB/s and NVLink at much higher, but those rates only apply with pinned (page-locked) host buffers. Pageable host memory — the default for any normal torch.Tensor created on the CPU — has to be staged through an internal pinned buffer inside the CUDA driver, which throttles transfer rates to ~2 GB/s. 

27 GB / 2 GB/s ≈ 13.5 s per direction × 2 directions = 27 s   ← matches the trace exactly

Two ways to make this go away

PathSavingEffort
Don't offload (already proved by Y4-no-offload) 27 s 0 — just drop --offload_model True. Requires 78 GB free per GPU; H100 80 GB fits.
Pin the CPU-resident DiT weights via tensor.pin_memory() 22-25 s ~10 LOC patch — pin both DiTs after their initial CPU-side load. Useful on tighter-memory GPUs (≤40 GB) where you can't keep both DiTs resident.

SGLang's --pin-cpu-memory flag does exactly the second approach for its --dit-layerwise-offload. That's part of why SGLang's offload-on configuration is fast: same algorithm, pinned memory instead of pageable.

8. Cross-step invariance check

All three captures — warmup (step 0), steady (steps 3-4), boundary (steps 25-26) — produce nearly identical GPU kernel breakdowns:

The DiT forward is a stationary workload across the loop. Whatever the per-step optimization picture is at step 3, it's the same at step 0 (warmup), step 26 (boundary), and presumably every other step. Anomalous step times (step 0's +9.4 s, step 26's +27 s) come from outside the GPU compute path — autotune and PCIe respectively. This is the cleanest possible answer to "do we need to optimize different steps differently?": no, the DiT forward is one workload.

10. Optimization implications — what to spend effort on

With the verified kernel decomposition in hand, the rank-ordered list of remaining lossless optimizations (and one lossy bonus) is:

#OptimizationTarget category Realistic savingCost / risk
1 Drop --offload_model True step-26 PCIe memcpy 27 s Lossless, already validated. Y4-no-offload's MD5 == Y4-sync's MD5. Use whenever GPU mem allows (H100 80 GB easily fits).
2 Build FA3 from source for Hopper attention math (59%) ~100-130 s Lossless. Build is gated on torch 2.11 + cu13 wheel/source compatibility — historically fragile. If it works, lifts attention from ~560 → ~800 TFLOPS sustained.
3 FP8 quantization on FFN matmul (lossy) matmul (20.3%, at 27% peak) ~150-200 s Out of this engagement's scope. Doubles bytes-per-FLOP, lifts nvjet kernels from ~205 → ~400 TFLOPS plausibly. Needs PSNR validation; quality impact varies by model.
4 Pin CPU-resident DiT weights step-26 PCIe memcpy (alternative to #1) ~22-25 s Lossless. ~10 LOC patch. Only relevant if you can't drop offload (smaller GPUs).
5 Kernel fusion via torch.compile elementwise (18.7%) ~30-60 s Currently gated on non-determinism bug; reduce-overhead mode produces different MD5s on different runs. If determinism is fixed, lifts elementwise efficiency by fusing ~14 small kernels into a few larger ones.
6 Fix the in-tree slow checkpoint loader pipeline_init (not in 613 s) ~15 min cold-start saved Lossless, no per-frame impact. Matters only for short-batch serving (see benchmark report).

The realistic remaining lossless ceiling

Adding items 1 + 2 + 5 (if compile determinism is solvable) to Y4-sync:

Configdiffusion_sSpeedup vs 1-GPU

After that, the headroom is in the lossy bucket (FP8, step distillation, TeaCache, sparse attention). The lossless ceiling on 2× NVLink-connected H100s for 1280×720×81 frames at 40 UniPC steps is approximately ~3.5-3.9× single-GPU, i.e. 415-470 s diffusion.
